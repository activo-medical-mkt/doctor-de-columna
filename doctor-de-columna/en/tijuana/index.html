Understand your spine

Spine conditions
we treat.

Learn about symptoms, diagnosis and treatment options for your spine condition.

01

Sciatica

Sciatic nerve pain and treatment options.

Sciatica is pain, tingling, numbness or weakness that usually runs down one leg, caused by irritation or compression of the nerve roots that form the sciatic nerve. Treatment depends on the cause, which your evaluation identifies.

Treatment options that may be considered

  • Physical therapy and activity adjusted to your symptoms.
  • Medication for pain and, in selected cases, injections.
  • Decompression surgery when clinically indicated.
02

Lumbar disc herniation

Learn about symptoms, causes and treatment options.

A lumbar disc herniation occurs when disc material in the lower back moves out of place and may irritate or compress a nerve root. It can cause lower back pain, leg pain, tingling or weakness. Your symptoms and imaging help guide treatment.

Treatment options that may be considered

  • Physical therapy, activity adjustments and medication for pain.
  • Epidural injections in selected cases.
  • Discectomy to relieve nerve compression when indicated.
03

Cervical disc herniation

Understand disc herniations in the neck.

A herniated disc in the neck can press on a nerve root and cause pain that spreads to the shoulder or arm, tingling or weakness. An exam and imaging show which area is involved and which options make sense for you.

Treatment options that may be considered

  • Physical therapy and an individualized neck exercise program.
  • Medication or injections when appropriate after specialist evaluation.
  • Decompression surgery when clinically indicated.
04

Spinal stenosis

Narrowing of the spinal canal and nerve compression.

Spinal stenosis is a narrowing of the space the spinal nerves pass through. In the lower back, it can cause leg pain, heaviness, tingling or weakness when you walk or stand. Care is based on your symptoms and how much they affect your daily life.

Treatment options that may be considered

  • Physical therapy and adapted exercise to improve function.
  • Medication for pain or injections in selected cases.
  • Surgical decompression when supported by your evaluation.
05

Adult scoliosis

Spinal curvature and postural imbalance.

Adult scoliosis is a sideways spinal curve that persists from adolescence or develops with age-related changes. It may cause pain, fatigue or postural imbalance. Treatment considers the curve, symptoms and how they change over time.

Treatment options that may be considered

  • Clinical follow-up, strengthening and posture exercises.
  • Medication for pain or injections in selected cases.
  • Corrective and stabilization surgery when indicated.
06

Spondylolisthesis

A slipped vertebra and lower back instability.

Spondylolisthesis occurs when one vertebra slips relative to the one below it. Some people have no symptoms; others experience lower back pain or nerve compression. The degree of slippage, stability and symptoms guide treatment.

Treatment options that may be considered

  • Physical therapy, strengthening and activity adjustments.
  • Medication for pain or injections, depending on your case.
  • Decompression and/or spinal fusion in selected cases.
07

Chronic lower back pain

Lower back pain that lingers or keeps coming back.

It has many possible causes, so your symptoms, exam and imaging are reviewed together.

Treatment options that may be considered

  • A check of your symptoms and nerve function.
  • A personalized physical therapy and medication plan.
  • Procedures or surgery when indicated.
08

Cervical radiculopathy

Pain, tingling or weakness that may extend into the arm.

It results from irritation or compression of a nerve root in the neck.

Treatment options that may be considered

  • A check of your symptoms and nerve function.
  • A personalized physical therapy and medication plan.
  • Procedures or surgery when indicated.

Treatment depends on an individual evaluation. These options are not appropriate for every patient.

Treatments and procedures

A treatment plan
for your spine.

See what your options are. The right treatment starts with your evaluation and diagnosis.

01

Endoscopic spine surgery

A minimally invasive approach for selected disc herniations and stenosis.

01

Endoscopic spine surgery

An endoscope allows the surgeon to view and treat selected spinal conditions through a small access point. Suitability depends on your diagnosis.

02

Microdiscectomy

Removing a disc fragment that presses on a nerve.

02

Microdiscectomy

A microsurgical approach removes the disc fragment compressing a nerve root. Your evaluation determines whether this is an appropriate option.

03

Lumbar decompression

Relieving pressure on nerves in the spinal canal.

03

Lumbar decompression

This procedure creates more room for the nerves in the lower back. It may be considered when nerve compression requires surgery.

04

Spinal fusion

Surgical stabilization of the spine.

04

Spinal fusion

Spinal fusion joins two or more vertebrae to stabilize part of the spine. Whether it is appropriate depends on your diagnosis, spinal stability and symptoms.

05

Epidural injections

Targeted injections for spine pain.

05

Epidural injections

Epidural injections deliver medication near the spinal nerves. They may be considered for pain management in selected cases.

06

Spine rehabilitation

Mobility and strength, with medical follow-up.

06

Spine rehabilitation

Guided exercise and regular follow-up to rebuild mobility, strength and stability, adapted to your diagnosis and stage of recovery.

Contact the clinic
01

How do I book a consultation in Tijuana?

You can message us on WhatsApp or call +52 664 159 6633. Visits are by appointment, and the clinic will confirm a time based on availability.

02

Where is the clinic?

The clinic is on the 27th floor of New City Medical Plaza, Av. Paseo del Centenario 9580, Zona Urbana Río Tijuana, B.C. 22010, Mexico, near the San Ysidro border crossing. View the map and directions.

03

What should I bring to my first appointment?

Bring any imaging and medical reports you already have, a list of your medications and your questions. For a second opinion, include details of your previous diagnosis and treatment.

04

Can I book a second opinion?

Yes. You can book an evaluation to review your previous diagnosis, your imaging and your options. Bring the reports and images from your earlier tests.

05

Does every spine condition require surgery?

No. Depending on your diagnosis, treatment may include rehabilitation, medication or other nonsurgical options. Treatment is chosen after an individual evaluation. Surgery is considered when clinically indicated.

06

How much does a consultation cost?

Ask about the current consultation fee and payment options via WhatsApp before confirming your appointment.

07

Can I use my health insurance?

Contact the clinic to discuss your insurer and any documents that may be needed. Coverage depends on your policy and any required authorization. Confirm cross-border coverage directly with your insurer.

08

How long does recovery take after spine surgery?

Recovery varies with the procedure, your health and the activities you want to return to. Your evaluation includes a discussion of recovery, follow-up appointments and a rehabilitation plan for your case.
