[01] · Understand your diagnosis

What is
adult scoliosis?

Scoliosis is a sideways curve of the spine, often with some rotation of the vertebrae. In adults it can be scoliosis that started in adolescence and never went away, or a new curve caused by wear and tear on the discs and joints, which is more common after age 50.

  • Many curves don’t cause pain.
  • Most are treated without surgery.
  • Treatment depends on your symptoms and your spinal balance.

Types of scoliosis

Types of
adult scoliosis

Adult scoliosis is classified by its cause. Knowing the type helps explain how it may change over time.

01 · Wear and tear
De novo
Starts
after age 50.

Degenerative scoliosis

Uneven wear on the discs and joints creates a new curve, almost always in the lower back. It can come with stenosis.

02 · Since adolescence
Idiopathic
Carries on
into adulthood.

Idiopathic scoliosis

A curve that began in adolescence and, in adulthood, may cause pain or progress with wear and tear.

03 · Other causes
Secondary
Less
common.

Secondary scoliosis

Linked to osteoporosis, previous surgery, a difference in leg length or other conditions. It’s assessed during your visit.

How scoliosis is measured

The curve and spinal balance are measured on a standing X-ray. These measurements guide the diagnosis, but treatment is decided by your symptoms.

MeasurementWhat it showsWhy it matters
Cobb angleHow many degrees the spine curves to one side.Scoliosis starts at 10 degrees; it helps track whether the curve is getting worse.
Sagittal balanceWhether your head lines up over your pelvis when you stand.Leaning forward is linked to more pain and fatigue when walking.
RotationWhether the vertebrae twist or slip against each other.It can narrow the space for the nerves and cause leg pain.
What you may feel

Symptoms of
adult scoliosis

Many curves cause no symptoms. When they do, it’s usually from wear and tear, muscle fatigue or pressure on the nerves. Explore each one to learn more.

01

Lower or
mid back pain

Pain that usually gets worse when you stand or walk and eases when you lie down, from muscle fatigue and joint wear.

02

Uneven shoulders
or hips

One shoulder higher than the other, one hip more prominent, or a feeling that you lean to one side or forward.

03

Pain that travels
down the leg

If the curve narrows the space around a nerve, you may get pain, tingling or numbness in the leg, similar to sciatica.

04

Tiring when
you walk

Fatigue or trouble standing up straight after walking for a while. If you notice new or worsening weakness, seek prompt care and review the emergency warning signs on this page.

Differential diagnosis

Conditions that can mimic
adult scoliosis

Spinal stenosis, spondylolisthesis and other conditions can cause similar symptoms or occur along with the curve. An evaluation tells them apart.

01

Spinal stenosis

Often occurs with degenerative scoliosis and causes leg symptoms when walking.

02

Sciatica

Pain down the leg when the curve or another cause compresses a nerve.

03
Same symptom.
Different cause.

Spondylolisthesis

A slipped vertebra can occur along with the curve and cause similar symptoms.

Your first evaluation

How is adult
scoliosis diagnosed?

Diagnosing adult scoliosis combines your medical history, a posture and nerve exam, and X-rays of the whole spine. Bring any imaging you already have.

See the clinic
  1. 01

    History and exam

    When you first noticed the curve or the pain and how it affects your walking, plus a check of posture, strength and sensation.

  2. 02

    Full-spine X-ray

    Taken standing up to measure the curve and the balance of the whole spine.

  3. 03

    MRI or CT scan

    If you have leg pain or treatment is being planned, they show the nerves and structure in more detail.

!

Adult scoliosis warning signs

Go to an emergency room right away if you lose bladder or bowel control, have numbness in the genitals or between the legs, have severe or worsening weakness in the legs or arms, or have new difficulty walking or using your hands.

A plan for your case

Adult scoliosis
treatment in Tijuana

Most people with adult scoliosis are treated without surgery: medication, physical therapy and, in selected cases, injections. Surgery is considered if pain or deformity limits your daily life despite treatment, if there are nerve symptoms or if the curve is progressing.

01 · Nonsurgical

Medical treatment

Pain and inflammation control with an individual plan and follow-up. Medications are reviewed with your doctor; avoid self-medicating.

Care and services
02 · Movement

Physical therapy

Strengthening, posture and conditioning exercises to reduce pain and keep you active.

Care and services
03 · Injections

Epidural injections

An injection near the irritated nerve to relieve leg pain in selected cases and make physical therapy easier.

Care and services
04 · Spine surgery

Decompression

Frees compressed nerves when leg pain is the main symptom and the curve is stable.

Learn about spine surgery
05 · Spine surgery

Fusion and correction

Stabilizes and corrects the curve with implants when deformity is progressing or imbalance limits your daily life.

Learn about spine surgery
06 · Spine surgery

Minimally invasive

Some procedures can be done through small incisions. Whether that’s an option depends on your anatomy, the curve and your overall health.

01

Does adult scoliosis always need surgery?

No. Most people control their symptoms with medication and physical therapy. Surgery is considered when pain or deformity limits your daily life despite treatment, when there are nerve symptoms or when the curve is progressing.

02

Can the curve keep getting worse?

Some curves progress slowly over time, especially degenerative ones. Follow-up X-rays let us monitor it and adjust treatment.

03

Can a brace correct scoliosis in adults?

In adults, a brace doesn’t correct the curve. In some cases it’s used for short periods to relieve pain, always under medical advice.

04

What tests do I need for my visit?

Bring any X-rays or MRIs you already have. A standing full-spine X-ray is usually ordered to measure the curve.

05

When should I go to the emergency room?

Go right away if you notice changes in bladder or bowel control, loss of feeling in the genitals or between the legs, severe or worsening weakness in the legs or arms, or new difficulty walking or using your hands.
