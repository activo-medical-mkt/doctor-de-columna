What is a
herniated disc?

A herniated disc happens when part of the soft center of a spinal disc pushes through its tougher outer ring and can irritate or press on a nerve root. Discs cushion movement between the vertebrae; herniations are most common in the lower back and the neck.

  • Many herniated discs improve without surgery.
  • A herniation on an MRI isn’t always the cause of the pain.
  • Treatment depends on your symptoms and exam.

Types of herniated disc by shape

On an MRI, a herniation is also described by how far the disc material extends. The term helps guide the diagnosis, but treatment is decided by your symptoms.

TypeWhat happens in the discWhat it usually means
ProtrusionThe disc bulges, but its outer ring is still intact.A common MRI finding, even in people with no pain.
ExtrusionThe center of the disc breaks through the outer ring into the spinal canal.More likely to irritate or compress a nerve root.
SequestrationA fragment of disc breaks off and sits free in the canal.It can cause intense symptoms; even so, the fragment is often reabsorbed over time.
What you may feel

Symptoms of a lumbar
and cervical herniated disc

Herniated disc symptoms depend on where the herniation is: in the lower back they’re usually felt in the leg, and in the neck, in the arm and hand. Explore each one to learn more.

01

Low back pain that
travels down the leg

A lumbar herniated disc can cause lower back pain that spreads to the buttock, leg or foot. This pattern is known as sciatica.

02

Neck pain that
travels down the arm

In the neck, a herniation can cause pain that spreads to the shoulder, arm or hand, sometimes with stiffness when you turn your head.

03

Tingling or
numbness

Pins and needles or loss of feeling in the leg, foot, arm or hand. Where it appears helps show which nerve is involved.

04

Weakness in the arm,
hand, leg or foot

Trouble gripping objects, lifting the front of your foot or climbing stairs needs to be evaluated. If it’s new or getting worse, seek prompt care and review the emergency warning signs on this page.

Your first evaluation

How is a herniated disc
diagnosed?

Diagnosing a herniated disc combines your medical history, a neurological exam and, when warranted, an MRI. Bring any imaging you already have.

See the clinic
  1. 01

    Your medical history

    Where the pain started, where it spreads and what makes it better or worse.

  2. 02

    Neurological exam

    Strength, reflexes and sensation to identify which nerve root is involved.

  3. 03

    MRI

    Confirms the location and size of the herniation when the exam findings warrant it.

!

Herniated disc warning signs

Go to an emergency room right away if you lose bladder or bowel control, have numbness in the genitals or between the legs, have severe or worsening weakness in the legs or arms, or have new difficulty walking or using your hands.

Herniated disc treatment
in Tijuana

Most herniated discs are treated without surgery first: medication, physical therapy and, in selected cases, injections. Surgery is considered if the pain doesn’t improve with that treatment or if there is weakness.

01 · Nonsurgical

Medical treatment

Pain and inflammation control with an individual plan and follow-up. Medications are reviewed with your doctor; avoid self-medicating.

Care and services
02 · Movement

Physical therapy

Guided exercises to restore mobility, strength and stability, and to lower the risk of future flare-ups.

Care and services
03 · Injections

Epidural injections

An injection near the affected nerve to relieve pain and inflammation in selected cases and make physical therapy easier.

Care and services
04 · Spine surgery

Endoscopic spine surgery

Removes the piece of disc pressing on the nerve through a small incision, using a camera. Whether it’s an option depends on your anatomy and diagnosis.

Learn about spine surgery
05 · Spine surgery

Microdiscectomy

Removes the part of the disc pressing on the nerve, using a microscope. It’s considered when there is a clear reason for surgery.

Learn about spine surgery
06 · Neck surgery

Cervical decompression

When a herniated disc in the neck compresses a nerve or the spinal cord, relieving that pressure may be considered. The technique is chosen case by case.

Learn about spine surgery

These options aren’t right for everyone. Your consultation covers the goals, alternatives and risks of each one.

01

Does a herniated disc always need surgery?

No. Most people improve with medication, physical therapy or injections. Surgery is considered when pain persists despite treatment, when there is weakness or when warning signs appear.

02

Can a herniated disc heal on its own?

Symptoms often ease over weeks or months with conservative treatment, and some of the disc material may be reabsorbed over time. Follow-up visits show whether the plan is working.

03

What’s the difference between a lumbar and a cervical herniated disc?

A lumbar herniation is in the lower back and usually causes pain that travels down the leg. A cervical herniation is in the neck and can cause pain, tingling or weakness in the arm and hand.

04

Do I need an MRI before my consultation?

Not necessarily. Bring any imaging you already have; your exam and medical history help decide whether a new MRI is needed.

05

When should I go to the emergency room?

Go right away if you notice changes in bladder or bowel control, loss of feeling in the genitals or between the legs, severe or worsening weakness in the legs or arms, or new difficulty walking or using your hands.
