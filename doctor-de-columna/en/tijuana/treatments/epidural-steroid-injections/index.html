[01] · What it is and what it aims to do

What is an
epidural steroid injection?

An epidural steroid injection delivers a corticosteroid, sometimes with a local anesthetic, into the space around the spinal nerves. Imaging guides the medication close to the irritated nerve root to reduce inflammation and pain.

  • Aims to relieve nerve pain.
  • Done with image guidance.
  • Doesn’t fix the structural cause of the compression.

Reference: AAOS · Epidural Steroid Injections.

may be considered.

A decision for your case

When is an epidural
injection considered?

It’s considered when pain persists despite initial treatment, to make physical therapy easier or to help confirm which nerve root is causing the symptoms.

Injection approaches

Types of
epidural injection.

The approach is chosen based on the affected level and where the irritated nerve root is.

01 · Specific nerve root

Transforaminal

The medication is directed to where a specific nerve root exits.

02 · Back of the canal

Interlaminar

Given between the laminae of the vertebrae, into the back of the epidural space.

03 · Base of the spine

Caudal

Given through the sacrum; may be considered for some problems low in the lumbar spine.

Preparation and follow-up

Before and after
your epidural injection.

It’s an outpatient procedure. It may take a few days to work, and how long it lasts varies from person to person.

01

Preparation

You’ll get instructions on fasting, which medications to adjust and bringing someone with you.

02

Procedure

Done with image guidance and local anesthesia. You’re monitored briefly afterward.

03

Follow-up

Your pain response is checked and your physical therapy plan is adjusted.

i

Risks and limitations of epidural injections.

Possible effects include pain at the injection site, a temporary increase in pain, headache, higher blood sugar or, less often, infection, bleeding or nerve injury. Relief may be partial or temporary, and the number of injections is limited.
